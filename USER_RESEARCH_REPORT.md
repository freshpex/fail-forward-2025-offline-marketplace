# AgriMarket User Research & Validation Report

## Research Overview

**Project:** AgriMarket - Offline-First Marketplace for Nigerian Farmers  
**Research Period:** November 26 - December 2nd, 2025  
**Methodology:** In-depth interviews, field observations, usability testing  
**Total Participants:** 15 farmers, 3 states  

---

## Executive Summary

This document presents findings from primary research conducted across Edo State, Ogun State, and Lagos State to validate the market opportunity for an offline-first agricultural marketplace. Research confirmed that smallholder farmers face significant barriers accessing digital marketplaces due to connectivity issues and are losing 40-60% of potential income to intermediaries.

---

## Research Methodology

### Participant Recruitment
- Contacted local agricultural extension officers
- Visited weekly markets in Benin City, Abeokuta, and Badagry
- Snowball sampling through farmer networks
- Selected farmers with varying tech literacy levels

### Interview Format
- Semi-structured interviews (45-60 minutes)
- Conducted in English, Pidgin, and Yoruba
- Audio recorded with consent
- Field observation at farms and markets

### Research Questions
1. How do farmers currently sell their produce?
2. What challenges do they face accessing markets?
3. What is their smartphone and internet usage?
4. How much income do they lose to middlemen?
5. Would they use a digital marketplace if it worked offline?

## Edo State Interviews

### Participant 1: Mr. Osahon Igbinoba
**Location:** Ekpoma, Esan West LGA  
**Age:** 47  
**Farm Size:** 3 hectares  
**Primary Crops:** Cassava, Plantain, Maize  
**Phone:** Tecno Spark 8 (Android)  

**Interview Date:** December 1st, 2025

**Key Quotes:**
> "The network here is very bad. Sometimes I travel 15km to Benin just to check my WhatsApp messages. By the time I return, the cassava buyer has already bought from someone else."

> "The middlemen know we are desperate. Last week I sold 50 bags of garri at ₦8,000 each. I found out later the market price in Benin was ₦15,000. That's almost half my money gone."

**Findings:**
- Sells 80% of produce to the same middleman who comes to the village
- Has smartphone but uses it mainly for calls due to poor network
- Willing to pay ₦500/month for a service that connects him to better buyers
- Loses approximately ₦180,000 monthly to price exploitation
- Post-harvest loss: ~20% (no storage, must sell quickly)

**Pain Points:**
- No real-time price information
- Middleman controls pricing
- Network only works at 2am-5am
- No access to buyers in Benin City

## Key Insights

### 1. Connectivity is the #1 Barrier
Every participant mentioned unreliable internet as their primary reason for not using existing digital platforms. Offline functionality is not a "nice to have" - it's essential.

### 2. Middlemen Capture 40-60% of Value
Consistent across all participants. This represents a massive opportunity for value redistribution to farmers.

### 3. Perishability Creates Urgency
Farmers with perishable goods (tomatoes, fish, vegetables) are most exploited due to time pressure. Speed of sale is critical.

### 4. Trust and Payment Security Matter
Multiple participants mentioned fear of scams. Payment protection and verified buyers are important features.

### 5. Logistics is as Important as Marketplace
Simply connecting buyers and sellers isn't enough. Integrated, affordable delivery is a key differentiator.

### 6. Willingness to Pay Exists
All participants said they would pay ₦500-2,000/month for a service that works. Transaction fees of 2-3% were considered acceptable.

### 7. Cooperative Potential
Several participants mentioned they could bring 10-20 other farmers if the platform works. Word of mouth will be primary acquisition channel.

---

## Recommendations

Based on this research, AgriMarket should:

1. **Prioritize offline-first architecture** - This is validated as essential, not optional
2. **Integrate logistics from day one** - Partner with Terminal Africa for affordable delivery
3. **Build trust mechanisms** - Verified sellers, buyer ratings, payment escrow
4. **Focus on perishables first** - Highest pain point, highest willingness to pay
5. **Enable cooperative selling** - Group features for farmer aggregation
6. **Keep it simple** - Large fonts, minimal text, vernacular support
7. **Seed Edo and Ogun first** - Existing relationships, then expand to Lagos buyers
